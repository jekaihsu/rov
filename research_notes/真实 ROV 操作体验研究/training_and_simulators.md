## 1. Competence frameworks (IMCA and other schemes)

### Takeaway
IMCA is the de facto framework for commercial offshore ROV crews: R 002 (introductory course requirements, Rev. 5, Aug 2025), C 005 (competence assurance by grade, logbook-evidenced), and, new in 2025, R 025 (remote-pilot introductory course) plus R 028 / G 014 (accreditation of "Class-A" ROV simulators, which are now required for IMCA-approved courses delivered in a virtual environment). Exact hour/day thresholds could only be obtained second-hand (100 logged supervised flying hours + 180 days offshore to advance), not from the primary documents.

### Cited Findings
- IMCA R 002 is titled "Requirements for IMCA-approved ROV introductory training courses", a Recommended Practice; current revision Rev. 5, August 2025; first published July 1996. It covers course purpose, approval, format, trainee admission requirements, learning objectives and syllabus, facilities/equipment, instructors, trainee assessment and certification; appendices: evidence portfolio, simulator portfolio, example trainee certificate, guide to live online training. (Fetched) — [IMCA technical library, R 002](https://www.imca-int.com/resources/technical-library/document/b0f5885b-c55b-ee11-8def-6045bdd0ecdb/)
- R 002 revision history shows the role of simulators and online delivery being actively reworked: Rev 4.5 (Feb 2024) removed online training from the options; Rev 4.6 (Jul 2024) amended hybrid delivery requirements; Rev 4.7 (Sep 2024) "added itemised simulator components"; Rev 4.8 (Nov 2024) removed the sole use of Class 1 & 2A from training equipment; Rev 4.9 (Mar 2025) added an IMCA guide to online training; Rev 5 (Aug 2025) added references to R 025 and the new simulator guidance. (Fetched) — [IMCA R 002 page](https://www.imca-int.com/resources/technical-library/document/b0f5885b-c55b-ee11-8def-6045bdd0ecdb/)
- IMCA R 025 "Requirements for IMCA-approved remote ROV pilot introductory training courses" was first published July 2025 (Rev. 0.1 Aug 2025); it prepares people for a remote ROV pilot role and includes a "simulator portfolio" appendix. (Fetched) — [IMCA R 025 page](https://www.imca-int.com/resources/technical-library/document/81a884aa-625d-f011-bec2-7c1e5202f828/)
- On 10 June 2025 IMCA published three documents: R 006 "ROV System Inspection" rev 2.0; R 028 "Requirements for IMCA-Approved Class-A ROV Simulator Accreditation" (new), defining Class-A simulators as "high-fidelity, fully immersive training systems" and setting minimum capabilities, performance benchmarks and evaluation procedures; and G 014 "Interim Scheme for IMCA Approval of Simulator Systems Used for Work and Skills", under which currently only ROV simulators can be approved (DP etc. planned). IMCA's technical adviser said the documents were requested by members and their clients. (Fetched) — [IMCA news, 10 Jun 2025](https://www.imca-int.com/news-events/imca-news/news/imca-publishes-new-guidance-for-rov-simulator-approval/)
- A Class-A simulator is required for approval of ROV introductory courses (R 002 and R 025) delivered in a virtual environment; simulator class criteria are defined in IMCA C 014 "Guidance on the use of simulators" (4th revision, 2015). (Snippet) — [IMCA approvals page](https://imca-int.com/certification/approvals)
- C 005 is "Guidance on competence assurance and assessment: Remote Systems & ROV division"; its revision history includes the addition of a "Remote Pilot grade II" and an October 2024 amendment of Section 6.3 to align C 004 and C 005. (Snippet) — [IMCA technical library, C 005](https://www.imca-int.com/resources/technical-library/document/3907f45c-c55b-ee11-8def-6045bdd2c7e3/)
- IMCA states the introductory course "should not be construed as achieving any level of competence defined in IMCA C005"; i.e. the course is an entry ticket, competence is assessed on the job. (Snippet) — [IMCA R 002 legacy page](https://legacy.imca-int.com/?p=28542)
- Advancement between grades: an older IMCA article says that, in addition to being assessed competent at the current level, a candidate must demonstrate 100 logged and supervised flying hours and 180 days working offshore; the ROV Personnel Logbook records maintenance, flying activities and competence. (Snippet; page returned HTTP 401 when fetched) — [IMCA legacy article](https://legacy.imca-int.com/?p=2490)
- A recruiter posting asks for "at least 180 days offshore experience as ROV Pilot Technician Grade II" and "at least 150 hours piloting", i.e. employers may set a higher bar than the 100 h figure. (Snippet) — [Elsa Talent job posting](https://careers-page.com/elsa-talent-solutions-sdn-bhd/job/L8VRXV7R)
- The IMCA ROV Personnel Logbook covers ROV Pilot/Technician I and II, Senior Pilot/Technician and Supervisor, and is to be completed in line with C 005. (Snippet) — [IMCA shop, ROV personnel logbook](https://shop.imca-int.com/product/logbooks/rov-personnel)
- A 2008 blog summary of IMCA guidance says a Supervisor needs competence as Senior Pilot/Technician plus at least one year's experience at that grade, an offshore medical and survival certificate (dated; may not match current C 005). (Snippet) — [rov-id blog, 2008](https://rov-id.blogspot.com/2008/09/competence-assurance-and-assessment-in.html)
- Manufacturer scheme (VideoRay): competencies are split into Knowledge Requirements KR-1 Vehicle Components, KR-2 Control Software, KR-3 Project Planning, KR-4 Data Processing, KR-5 Diagnostics, and Skill Proficiencies PR-1 Computer Use, PR-2 Operational Tactics, PR-3 Maintenance, presented as a checkbox list with core vs advanced items; followed by an hours-of-operation log book with separate columns for Pilot, Tether Handler, Accessory Operations, Maintenance and Supervision. Exercise sets are "Manual Control", "Auto Modes" and "DP & Jog Controls". (Fetched) — [VideoRay competencies](https://download.videoray.com/documentation/vtl/html/competencies.html)
- VideoRay describes its Basic Operator Certificate as "like a vehicle driver's license". (Snippet) — [VideoRay MSS training docs](https://download.videoray.com/documentation/mss/html/trn_basic_op_pro_4.php)
- Deep Trekker runs per-vehicle online courses (Pivot, Photon, Revolution) with modules on equipment/batteries, setup, first operation and optional sonar gear, ending in a pilot certificate of completion. (Snippet) — [Deep Trekker Training](https://training.deeptrekker.com/p/photon)

### Inferences
- IMCA competence is evidence-based (logbook hours + supervisor sign-off against C 005 tables), not exam-based. A training simulator aimed at company operators should therefore produce exportable, per-session evidence (hours flown, tasks completed, conditions, pass/fail against criteria) that maps onto a logbook, rather than only an in-game score.
- The 2024-2025 IMCA churn (simulator components itemised, Class-A accreditation, remote-pilot course) shows that simulator hours are becoming formally creditable in ROV training, but only for accredited "Class-A" systems. A consumer-grade product should not claim IMCA equivalence; it can align its exercise vocabulary and logging with the IMCA structure.
- VideoRay's logbook separating Pilot / Tether Handler / Supervision hours signals that crew roles other than stick time are part of recognised competence.

### Gaps
- The actual C 005 competence tables (what a Pilot Technician Grade II must demonstrate, per-grade criteria) and the R 002 syllabus tables with hours could not be read: IMCA pages only expose abstracts; the rovworld.com mirror of R 002 returned HTTP 522. The 100 h / 180 day figure is second-hand and from an older article; verify against the current C 005 PDF.
- R 028 Class-A simulator technical criteria (what must be modelled, latency/fidelity benchmarks) are not public on the news page; not obtained.
- No information found on MTS ROV Committee certification, national vocational qualifications (e.g. SQA/NVQ), public-safety-specific ROV operator curricula, Blue Robotics partner courses, or any QYSEA operator training programme. Searches returned nothing usable for these.

## 2. Training curricula of pilot schools and manufacturer courses

### Takeaway
Commercial ROV schools run roughly 3-7 week courses dominated by technician content (electrics, hydraulics, high voltage, LARS, maintenance), with comparatively little stick time; manufacturer courses for small vehicles are 2-5 days and centre on system operation, tether deployment, sonar, manipulator and navigation exercises.

### Cited Findings
- The Underwater Centre (Fort William) work-class course (2014 launch) covered: ROV industry familiarisation, electrical and electronic systems, a two-day high-voltage awareness course, hydraulics, working at height, ROV operations with launch and recovery, piloting an observation-class ROV and a Triton XL26 in operational conditions, and maintenance such as retermination of armoured umbilicals. (Snippet) — [Engineer Live](https://www.engineerlive.com/node/8846)
- That course was originally five weeks; Technip supplied a Triton XL32 and a TXL simulator for it. (Snippet) — [Press and Journal](https://pressandjournal.co.uk/fp/news/327460/training-rov-operators-meet-demand)
- A later revamp describes a three-week ROV Pilot Technician Course (adding time on the Loch Sunart ROV vessel and workshop training) and a seven-week Premium ROV Course. (Snippet) — [Ocean News & Technology](https://oceannews.com/?p=40748)
- One graduate's CV lists about 12 flying hours and about 40 operational hours on a Seaeye Falcon plus 2 hours on a Triton XL simulator from such a course (single individual, not an official figure). (Snippet) — [SlideShare CV](https://www.slideshare.net/StuartBiegala/sbrovcv)
- A general career guide states most formal ROV pilot training programmes run three to seven weeks, combining theory, simulation and hands-on experience, and that employers look for IMCA benchmarks. (Snippet) — [Deep Trekker, "ROV pilot"](https://www.deeptrekker.com/news/rov-pilot)
- VideoRay Mission Specialist ROV Operator Course (2026 description): generally 3-5 days, classroom plus on-water, delivered at the customer site or at Pottstown PA / San Diego CA. Topics and practical exercises: ROV system overview; modules; accessories; Greensea IQ control software; tether deployment system; manipulator operation; sonar operation; accessory setup; charts overview and exercise; markers and waypoints; creating missions; manipulator and sonar exercises; positioning/navigation; autonomous missions; diagnostics and troubleshooting; system maintenance/repairs; module replacement. (Fetched PDF) — [VideoRay ROV Operator Course Description 2026](https://videoray.com/wp-content/uploads/ROV_Operator_Course_Description-2026.pdf)
- VideoRay published Pro 4 class pricing of USD 2,690 for a 2-day class and USD 3,540 for a 3-day class; sonar and USBL positioning can each be added as an extra day. (Snippet) — [VideoRay Pro 4 Basic Operator Course](https://download.videoray.com/documentation/mss/html/trn_basic_op_pro_4.php)
- OpenROV field training (Papua New Guinea, 2014): after one day there were "23 skilled pilots"; one exercise had each student pilot around a tank "without looking at the tank or robot" (camera feed only), described as challenging "even for veteran OpenROV pilots"; students ran the complete pre-dive start-up routine unaided. (Fetched) — [Southern Fried Science, A. Thaler, 10 Nov 2014](https://southernfriedscience.com/?p=17896)

### Inferences
- Stick time in entry courses is scarce (order of 10-15 flying hours in a multi-week course, per the single CV data point), which is the gap simulators are sold to fill.
- Curricula treat piloting as one strand among system knowledge, pre-dive checks, launch/recovery, tether handling, sonar/navigation, manipulator work, and fault diagnosis. A training product that only teaches flying covers a minority of the syllabus; pre-dive checklists, launch/recovery and fault diagnosis are natural non-flying modules.
- "Fly on camera only, no direct view of the vehicle" is used as an explicit, early training step even for hobby-grade vehicles.

### Gaps
- No current (2025-2026) syllabus with hour allocations was obtained for The Underwater Centre, MTCS, QSTAR, Subnet/Abyss or ROVOP academy; the Underwater Centre details are from 2014-2016 trade press snippets.
- No source found for sea-time exercise lists or for emergency-procedure drills at commercial schools.

## 3. Standard pilot skill exercises and how they are scored

### Takeaway
The best-documented scoring scheme found is the TRANSoM tutor: five manoeuvre phases (descent, transit, hover, orbit, dock) scored on six skills using continuously sampled error "sensors" with thresholds calibrated against expert pilots, with separate thresholds for current vs no current. VideoRay's manual-control exercises (hold station, follow tether, managing tether) show that tether effects are embedded in even the most basic pool drills. MATE competition scoring is points per task plus time bonus and penalties.

### Cited Findings
- TRANSoM curriculum (ONR-funded; Imetrix, BBN, MIT, Boeing): trainee practises descent, transit, hover, orbit and dock under a computer coach; lessons follow demonstrate -> practise -> review, with replay, expert-path comparison and re-flying of segments where errors occurred. (Fetched PDF) — [Fletcher & Roberts, I/ITSEC 1998](https://xcdacademy.s3.amazonaws.com/iitsec/1998/72757R4N63405774.pdf)
- TRANSoM score matrix: five phases (Descent, Transit, Hover, Orbit, Dock) by six skills (Stay On Course, Steady Progress, Drive Smoothly, Face the Target, Avoid Collisions, Snapshots), each cell shown green/yellow/red for good/fair/poor. (Fetched PDF) — [Fletcher & Roberts 1998](https://xcdacademy.s3.amazonaws.com/iitsec/1998/72757R4N63405774.pdf)
- Underlying metrics, sampled at least once per second and summarised as RMS error, min/max error and percentage of time outside a threshold: Path sensor (horizontal deviation from expert path, m); Bearing sensor (angle between heading and bearing to target, deg); Depth sensor (depth vs expert path depth); Hover sensor (straight-line distance from hover point); Range sensor (deviation from a prescribed range such as orbit distance); Speed sensor (lower and upper thresholds); Control/Smoothness sensor (variance of commanded thrust over a time window, plus zero crossings and stick reversals per axis); Progress sensor (velocity component toward target during transit). (Fetched PDF) — [Fletcher & Roberts 1998](https://xcdacademy.s3.amazonaws.com/iitsec/1998/72757R4N63405774.pdf)
- Thresholds: 59 pairs of good/fair boundaries were set from expert and intermediate pilots flying 12 tasks; separate thresholds were needed for current vs no-current because current "greatly affects the difficulty"; expert scores varied widely and novices sometimes matched experts in no-current cases; some thresholds were later loosened so the slowest learners could progress. (Fetched PDF) — [Fletcher & Roberts 1998](https://xcdacademy.s3.amazonaws.com/iitsec/1998/72757R4N63405774.pdf)
- TRANSoM test tasks on a real ROV in a 6.8 m x 10 m x 4.3 m pool: "Barrel Task" (turn, descend, transit, hover, orbit, dock around a barrel target) and "Inspection Task" (pipe inspection: face the structure while moving along it reading numbers/letters attached at irregular intervals). Conditions were graded: no current / 3.5 m visibility; 0.07 m/s current / 1.5 m visibility; 0.14 m/s current / 1.5 m visibility. Inspection was scored by range RMSE from the pipe and an inspection-speed measure combining velocity and reading accuracy. (Fetched PDF) — [Fletcher & Roberts 1998](https://xcdacademy.s3.amazonaws.com/iitsec/1998/72757R4N63405774.pdf)
- VideoRay "Hold Station" exercise: navigate along a pool wall, turn toward the wall and hold on a point of interest (filter inlet, light); "Tether 'recoil' will tend to pull the ROV in the direction from where you started" and yaw it; remedies are to overshoot then reverse back (adds slack) or ask the tether handler to deploy more tether; "usually more than one correct way". (Fetched) — [VideoRay hold station exercise](https://download.videoray.com/training/vtl_pro_5_exercises_manual_control/html/_hold_station_narration.html)
- VideoRay calls finding and following the tether "one of the most critical skills an ROV pilot needs to master"; an exercise has pilots fly out, turn around and follow the tether back as closely as possible; other exercises in the set include "Managing Tether" and a Defender "Tether Mismanagement" exercise. (Snippet) — [VideoRay follow tether exercise](https://download.videoray.com/training/vtl_pro_5_exercises_manual_control/html/_follow_tether_narration.html)
- VideoRay snag guidance: "Do NOT Pull the Tether!"; pilot the vehicle back out. (Snippet) — [VideoRay emergency snag procedure](https://download.videoray.com/documentation/mss/html/emergency_snag.html)
- Forum's VMAX standard scenario library is described as designed "to train and evaluate ROV pilots in basic and complex skills such as tether management, access evaluation, and manipulator control". (Snippet) — [Forum Energy Technologies VMAX page](https://f-e-t.com/?p=2974)
- MATE ROV Competition scoring components (all classes SCOUT/NAVIGATOR/RANGER/PIONEER/EXPLORER): product demonstration (in-water tasks), technical documentation, engineering presentation, marketing display, safety inspection, company spec sheet and corporate responsibility; upper classes add a float test, JSEA and workmanship sheets. (Fetched) — [MATE ROV Competition scoring page](https://materovcompetition.org/scoring)
- Older MATE rule details: 2017 SCOUT time bonus of 1 point per minute and 0.01 point per second under 15 minutes; 5-point penalty for diver assistance; penalties for debris left in pool. (Snippet) — [2017 SCOUT product demo scoresheet](https://www.marinetech.org/files/marine/files/ROV%20Competition/2017%20competition/Judges/2017%20SCOUT%20Product%20Demo%20Scoresheet%20FINAL_2.pdf)
- 2019 MATE briefing: time bonus only if all tasks were completed perfectly with no penalties; Explorer class allowed one replacement fuse, a second blown fuse ended the run. (Snippet) — [MATE team briefing 2019](https://www.marinetech.org/files/marine/files/ROV%20Competition/2019%20competition/Missions/Team%20Briefing%202019.pdf)

### Inferences
- A defensible scoring model for a training mode: per-phase x per-skill matrix with traffic-light grading, driven by RMS error / percent-time-out-of-tolerance metrics (path, heading-to-target, depth, hover radius, stand-off range, speed band, control smoothness via stick reversals, collisions), thresholds calibrated on expert runs, and separate thresholds per current condition.
- Time should be a secondary or gated metric (MATE gates the time bonus on a perfect, penalty-free run; TRANSoM asks for speed "without sacrificing accuracy").
- Canonical exercise ladder supported by sources: descend -> transit -> hover/hold station -> orbit a target facing it -> dock; then pipe/wall inspection at fixed stand-off; then tether drills (follow tether home, hold station against tether recoil, snag recovery without pulling); then sonar/manipulator tasks; each repeated with stronger current and lower visibility.
- Tether turn counting was not found as an explicit published metric in any fetched source, although tether management is named as a skill everywhere.

### Gaps
- The 2026/2027 MATE scoring spreadsheets and mission manuals (point values, task lists) were not opened; only category names verified.
- No published IMCA or school assessment sheet with numeric pass criteria was found.
- VideoRay's full exercise list and any pass standards were not retrieved (only index titles and one exercise).

## 4. Professional and open-source simulators

### Takeaway
Commercial work-class simulators (Forum VMAX, Fugro DeepWorks, GRi VROV) all advertise force-based vehicle physics, dynamic tether/cable models, current, sonar and manipulator simulation, scenario editors, record/replay, fault injection and coupling to real pilot consoles; none publishes pricing or validation data. Open-source research simulators (Stonefish, DAVE, HoloOcean, MARUS, UNav-Sim) target autonomy research, not pilot training: no instructor station, no scoring, and tether support is not documented in the main comparative review.

### Cited Findings
Commercial:
- Forum VMAX: features listed include "dynamic cable system and realistic object hydro dynamic physics", complex material and adjustable friction system, realistic visuals and shadowing "showing accurate depth perception", in-scenario vessel and crane dynamics, real-time mission recording for playback, and a "VMAX Project Editor" for building in-house scenarios; delivered either as a laptop package for engineering or with a Forum ROV console for training; bespoke scenarios are a paid service. (Snippet) — [Forum Energy Technologies VMAX page](https://f-e-t.com/?p=2974)
- VMAX is used both to train/evaluate pilots and by engineers to verify installation/maintenance procedures and access; many scenarios require a 7-function manipulator; the manipulator control interface is called "key" to realism; runs on laptops (paired with BluHaptics Dex-OS in 2018). The article gives no physics detail, validation or limitations. (Fetched) — [Marine Technology News, Mar 2018](https://www.marinetechnologynews.com/news/simulation-meets-automation-enhance-557930)
- In 2019 Forum added eROV (electric vehicle) simulator training to VMAX. (Snippet) — [OE Digital](https://www.oedigital.com/news/464941-forum-unveils-erov-simulator-training)
- Fugro DeepWorks: "full force-modeled physics" so ROVs and movable subsea assets respond to operator demands, environmental forces and friction; DeepWorks Engineer runs on a desktop PC for planning; whole dataset and 3-D world are recorded and can be rewound to any point for review. (Snippet) — [Hart Energy / E&P](https://hartenergy.com/exclusives/rov-simulation-training-tool-cuts-operational-costs-and-risks-18642)
- DeepWorks 3D current profiles: operator defines strength, heading and elevation of currents at different coordinates and depths, changeable mid-mission; profiles act on the ROV tether as it passes through different current fields and around objects. (Snippet) — [Hydro International](https://www.hydro-international.com/content/news/simulator-models-complex-sea-currents)
- DeepWorks electric-ROV upgrade (2014): console feedback for Water Detection, Ground Detect and Line Impedance Monitor faults on circuits and sensors; Saab Seaeye garage (TMS) model for docking/undocking training. (Snippet) — [Ocean News & Technology](https://oceannews.com/?p=39096)
- DeepWorks was dynamically linked to a Kongsberg vessel/crane simulator so that if the crane operator winches the umbilical unexpectedly "the pilot sees his ROV pulled in response" (team training of launch/recovery). (Snippet; the exact article among the trade-press results was not isolated) — [OE Digital "Current events"](https://www.oedigital.com/news/459228-current-events)
- Industry rationale quoted for simulators: experienced pilots fly the critical operations so trainee "stick time" is restricted; "More and more oil companies are stipulating that a dry run must be carried out in the simulator before a new or challenging operation" (simulator-industry source). (Snippet) — [Hart Energy / E&P](https://hartenergy.com/exclusives/rov-simulation-training-tool-cuts-operational-costs-and-risks-18642)
- GRi Simulations VROV: Instructor's Control Console (ICC) lets the instructor monitor a trainee in real time and change cameras, lights, manipulators and environment (currents, turbidity, tether effects); "user-defined fault initiation to develop a pilot's crisis handling abilities"; "VROV Objective Overlay" to ensure completion of training objectives; dynamic tether modelling with collision detection; sonar "based on signal simulation with multi-beam compatibility"; a Teledyne SeaBotix variant with BlueView multibeam sonar. (Snippet) — [GRi VROV overview](https://grisim.kayako.com/article/19-virtual-remotely-operated-vehicle-overview)
- Marine Simulation ROVsim Pro (announced Dec 2008): modular applications communicating over a network; up to 25 user-configurable digital/analog inputs so most USB HID devices can be used; dedicated sonar application with multiple simultaneous sonar displays; later support for multiple ROVs in one mission and one instructor monitoring a group of vehicles. (Snippet) — [MarineLink](https://www.marinelink.com/news/simulation-introduces329008)
- TRANSoM (research prototype, 1990s) modelled inertia, response time, drag, maximum speeds, currents, visibility and particulates, scanning sonar, nav plot and tether paid-out readout; used the same physical controls for simulator and real ROV. (Fetched PDF) — [Fletcher & Roberts 1998](https://xcdacademy.s3.amazonaws.com/iitsec/1998/72757R4N63405774.pdf)

Open source (from Aldhaheri et al., OCEANS 2025 review, fetched):
- Stonefish (2019): Bullet physics + OpenGL, "advanced hydrodynamics", XML scenarios; manipulators, multi-vehicle and acoustic comms supported; forward-looking sonar; recent additions of event-based, thermal and optical-flow cameras; C++ library with ROS interface; Linux/Windows/macOS. — [arXiv 2504.06245](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06245v1)
- DAVE (2022): Gazebo-based, builds on UUV Simulator and WHOI ds_sim; imaging and forward-looking sonar; manipulators and multi-vehicle supported, no acoustic comms; ROS1, Linux only; ROS1 base flagged as obsolescence risk. — [arXiv 2504.06245](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06245v1)
- HoloOcean (2022): Unreal Engine 4.27 (pre-release UE 5.3) + PhysX; realistic imaging sonar, DVL; no manipulator support; multi-vehicle and acoustic comms; pip install; ROS2 wrapper. — [arXiv 2504.06245](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06245v1)
- MARUS (2022): Unity3D with a Dynamic Water Physics asset; multibeam and forward-looking sonar; no manipulator or multi-vehicle; ROS1/ROS2 via gRPC. — [arXiv 2504.06245](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06245v1)
- UNav-Sim (2023): Unreal Engine 5.1 + AirSim "Fast Physics"; vision-focused; no manipulator, multi-vehicle or acoustic comms; ROS1/ROS2. — [arXiv 2504.06245](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06245v1)
- Review conclusions: the sim-to-real gap persists; high fidelity is computationally expensive (realism vs efficiency trade-off); realistic disturbances such as sensor inaccuracies and fluctuating currents are essential; lack of standardisation and benchmarking. The review does not report tether modelling for any of the five. — [arXiv 2504.06245](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06245v1)
- SMaRCSim authors' critique: Stonefish has high visual fidelity but targets real-time single-vehicle simulation and (per them) lacked ROS 2 support; HoloOcean has proprietary, hard-to-modify scenes and single-agent ROS 2 support. (Snippet) — [SMaRCSim, arXiv 2506.07781](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07781v1)

### Inferences
- Table-stakes instructor features across VMAX / DeepWorks / VROV: live monitoring, changing environment mid-run (current, turbidity), fault injection (ground fault, water ingress, telemetry), scenario editor, full-state record and rewind, objective tracking. Published material is thin on automatic numeric scoring; evaluation appears to rely on instructor judgment plus objective checklists.
- Commercial simulators sell twice: pilot training and engineering "access/procedure rehearsal". The second use depends on importing a client's real field geometry.
- Real-console coupling (same hand controller as the vehicle) is treated as central by both vendors and TRANSoM. For a small-vehicle simulator this means supporting the actual controller and its mapping.
- No open-source simulator is aimed at human pilot training; an instructor/scoring layer and tether-centric exercises are differentiators.

### Gaps
- No public pricing found for VMAX, DeepWorks, VROV or ROVsim.
- Oceaneering/Schilling simulators, Abyssal, Kongsberg ROV simulation, EIVA, MarineVerse: not researched to source level in this session; no verified claims.
- Vendor feature claims are marketing statements from 2008-2019 trade press; no independent validation of tether or hydrodynamic fidelity was found, and no user criticism of these products was found in public sources.
- The GRi knowledge-base page and ICC manual could not be fetched (HTTP 404), so VROV details rest on search snippets.
- UUV Simulator is only mentioned as DAVE's base; Gazebo tether plugins not investigated.

## 5. Consumer games and Steam titles (kept separate from industrial training)

### Takeaway
The only dedicated ROV-piloting title found on Steam is subROV: Underwater Discoveries (sqr3lab, USD 19.99, Early Access since 2 Dec 2022, about 90-93% positive on only around 63 reviews). Press praise centres on authenticity, education and discovery; criticism centres on a slow, tutorial-heavy start, complex controls, slow vehicle movement and sparse early content.

### Cited Findings
- subROV store page: USD 19.99 (soundtrack bundle USD 26.98); released into Early Access 2 Dec 2022 and still Early Access; developer/publisher sqr3lab; "Very Positive", 90% of 63 user reviews positive; single-player; English only; Steam Deck "playable" with frame rate often in the 20s on the seafloor. Player pilots a full-ocean-depth ROV from a research vessel to chart the seafloor, study species, explore wrecks and sample hydrothermal vents using a scanner, articulated arm, sensors, lander and sediment corers, progressing from trainee to "Dive Master" with a discoveries Gallery. ## 6. Evidence on simulator training effectiveness and which fidelity dimensions matter

### Takeaway
The one controlled ROV study found (TRANSoM, 32 subjects, real-ROV post-test) shows that about 4.5 hours of simulator practice transfers to a real vehicle about as well as 4.5 hours on the real vehicle, including to an untrained inspection task. Structured tutoring did not beat free practice and did nothing for low-ability trainees, apparently because gated progression kept them away from hard current/low-visibility scenarios. Public evidence is otherwise thin and old.

### Cited Findings
- Design: 32 subjects (age 19-39, 65% MIT students) randomly assigned, 8 per group, to Full Training (VE + intelligent tutor), Simulation Only (free play, no feedback), ROV Only (free practice on the real ROV) or No Training; stick time matched at 4.5 hours (four 1.5 h sessions or 2-3 days); pre- and post-tests on the real Talon ROV in a pool. (Fetched PDF) — [Fletcher & Roberts, I/ITSEC 1998](https://xcdacademy.s3.amazonaws.com/iitsec/1998/72757R4N63405774.pdf)
- Result: on the untrained Inspection task, all three trained groups significantly outperformed No Training on range RMSE and inspection speed, reported lower workload, and approached the performance of two expert pilots. (Fetched PDF) — [Fletcher & Roberts 1998](https://xcdacademy.s3.amazonaws.com/iitsec/1998/72757R4N63405774.pdf)
- For most pre/post measures on the practised task (speed, path, depth) there were no significant differences between groups; authors cite the confined tank and "the generally rapid initial development of ROV piloting skills" (a practice effect visible even in the control group). Significant effects appeared only in the orbit phase (bearing and range RMSE), where Simulation Only and ROV Only improved. (Fetched PDF) — [Fletcher & Roberts 1998](https://xcdacademy.s3.amazonaws.com/iitsec/1998/72757R4N63405774.pdf)
- Anomaly: low-initial-ability subjects in the Full Training group showed no improvement in orbit. Explanations offered: mastery-gated progression meant 3 of 8 did not reach the hardest levels (high current, low visibility), whereas self-directed groups "elected to spend the majority of their time in the more difficult scenarios"; or verbal feedback created dependence or rewarded the wrong behaviour. (Fetched PDF) — [Fletcher & Roberts 1998](https://xcdacademy.s3.amazonaws.com/iitsec/1998/72757R4N63405774.pdf)
- Aptitude: visualisation aptitude (and to a lesser extent manual dexterity and spatial orientation) predicted performance on novel tasks for all groups; after Simulation Only or ROV practice, aptitude differences were largely eliminated on practised tasks. (Fetched PDF) — [Fletcher & Roberts 1998](https://xcdacademy.s3.amazonaws.com/iitsec/1998/72757R4N63405774.pdf)
- Fidelity validation: steady-state response and cross-coupling (heading rate under lateral input) matched the real vehicle; the simulation's time response measured faster than the real vehicle but pilots perceived it as slower, attributed to the slower visual update rate. Expert pilots rated displays, controls (identical hardware) and vehicle model "effective" or "highly effective". (Fetched PDF) — [Fletcher & Roberts 1998](https://xcdacademy.s3.amazonaws.com/iitsec/1998/72757R4N63405774.pdf)
- Augmented viewpoints: an external "stadium" view and an unobstructed ROV-centred view improved performance during training; the stadium view was most useful; removing them at test produced no negative effect, i.e. no dependence. The paper stresses that a key skill is "being able to operate the system with limited sensor information". (Fetched PDF) — [Fletcher & Roberts 1998](https://xcdacademy.s3.amazonaws.com/iitsec/1998/72757R4N63405774.pdf)
- A 2024 maritime-simulation study notes a continuing lack of research on transferability of simulator training to real-world scenarios. (Snippet) — [Virtual Reality (Springer), 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10055-024-01058-0)

### Inferences
- Fidelity priorities supported by the evidence: (1) correct steady-state dynamics and axis cross-coupling; (2) the same physical control interface; (3) display/frame update rate, because low visual update rate distorts perceived responsiveness even when dynamics are correct; (4) current and visibility as the main difficulty drivers. Photo-realism was not what made the 1990s system transfer.
- Do not hard-gate difficulty behind mastery: trainees locked out of hard conditions gained least. Let learners self-select current and visibility, and use scoring as feedback rather than as a lock.
- A third-person / external "ghost" view is a legitimate training aid and did not create dependence when withdrawn; it also serves the game audience. It should be removable for assessed runs.
- Initial piloting skill is acquired fast (a practice effect within a handful of trials), so basic manoeuvre content will be exhausted quickly; long-term value has to come from harder conditions and task variety.

### Gaps
- No recent (post-2000) controlled transfer study for ROV piloting was found; nothing on latency thresholds for ROV teleoperation, VR vs flat screen, or work-class simulator outcome data. Analogous-domain evidence (UAV, surgical, crane teleoperation) was not researched.
- TRANSoM used small groups (n = 8) of students in a small pool; effect sizes are only shown graphically.
- The companion paper "VE-based Training for Situation Awareness" (Fletcher, Roberts, Pioch, Perrin, Barnett) was identified by citation only.

## 7. What pilots find hardest and what simulators miss

### Takeaway
Sourced material points to tether behaviour (recoil, entanglement, following the tether home), operating on limited sensor information (camera-only view, low visibility, sonar), building a mental map, and current as the hard parts. First-hand pilot forum testimony on simulator shortcomings could not be accessed, so that part of the question is largely unanswered.

### Cited Findings
- VideoRay training material calls tether entanglement or separation the leading cause of lost ROVs or mission damage. (Snippet) — [VideoRay piloting tactics](https://download.videoray.com/documentation/mss/html/pilot_tactics.html)
- Tether recoil pulls and yaws a small ROV away from a held position, and the pilot must counter it or coordinate with the tether handler for more slack. (Fetched) — [VideoRay hold station exercise](https://download.videoray.com/training/vtl_pro_5_exercises_manual_control/html/_hold_station_narration.html)
- In the TRANSoM study, the only instructor intervention needed for self-directed real-ROV practice was "in extreme cases of tether entanglement". (Fetched PDF) — [Fletcher & Roberts 1998](https://xcdacademy.s3.amazonaws.com/iitsec/1998/72757R4N63405774.pdf)
- r/rov advice to a prospective pilot includes learning to build a mental "map" of the working environment and to "learn to fly with just the sonar". (Snippet; thread itself not reachable) — [r/rov thread mirror](https://red.bismuth.it/r/rov/comments/xkeafd/potential_rov_pilot_asking_for_advice/iq9jbhi/?context=3)
- TRANSoM authors: operating with limited sensor information is a key skill; the pilot's visual attention is divided among several displays, hence coaching was delivered by voice; current "greatly affects the difficulty" to the point of needing separate performance thresholds. (Fetched PDF) — [Fletcher & Roberts 1998](https://xcdacademy.s3.amazonaws.com/iitsec/1998/72757R4N63405774.pdf)
- Flying purely from the camera feed without sight of the vehicle is hard even for experienced hobby pilots. (Fetched) — [Southern Fried Science, 2014](https://southernfriedscience.com/?p=17896)
- An OpenROV has no built-in tether management; "operators must design their own and adapt it to the unique challenges of their field environment". (Fetched) — [Southern Fried Science, 2014](https://southernfriedscience.com/?p=17896)
- Team dimension: the DeepWorks-Kongsberg link was built so crane/winch operator errors pull the pilot's ROV; VideoRay logs Tether Handler and Supervision hours separately from Pilot hours. (Snippet / Fetched) — [OE Digital](https://www.oedigital.com/news/459228-current-events); [VideoRay competencies](https://download.videoray.com/documentation/vtl/html/competencies.html)

### Inferences
- For small tethered vehicles the tether is an active force on the vehicle (recoil, drag, snag), not only a length limit; a simulator that models tether tension and yaw moment and includes a "tether handler" (AI or second player paying out/taking in) would cover a skill that sources describe as critical and that the consumer competitor lacks.
- Exercises that deliberately restrict information (camera-only, low visibility, sonar-only navigation, no external view) match what trainers do in practice.
- Voice or audio coaching fits the task better than on-screen text because the pilot's eyes are saturated.

### Gaps
- No accessible first-hand statements from working pilots about what commercial simulators get wrong (tether feel, depth cues, disorientation, 2D-video situational awareness). r/rov (red.bismuth.it mirror) is outside this session's allowed network domains; it would need to be allowed, or the threads supplied, to close this gap. rovworld.com was down (HTTP 522).
- No sourced evidence on depth-perception aids (lasers, shadows) or disorientation in pilots beyond the VMAX marketing mention of shadowing for depth perception.
