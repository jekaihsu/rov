## 1. Underwater optics: colour absorption, visibility ranges, turbidity, green vs blue water

### Takeaway
Water removes colour in spectral order (red first, then orange, yellow), and the loss depends on the total light path (surface to subject to lens, or lamp to subject to lens), not only depth; published depth figures vary widely with water clarity. Quantitative per-water-type visibility figures were only found for the worst case (turbid port water, under 1 m) and for very turbid Jerlov coastal types (veiling-light distance reached at 4-5 m).

### Cited Findings
- Colours disappear underwater in the same order as they appear in the spectrum; longer wavelengths are absorbed first. One oceanography text gives: red absorbed in the upper 10 m, orange by about 40 m, yellow gone before 100 m. — [Introduction to Oceanography, 6.5 Light](https://pressbooks.ccconline.org/introduction-to-oceanography/chapter/6-5-light/); [Webb, Introduction to Oceanography](https://rwu.pressbooks.pub/webboceanography/?p=195)
- A diving reference puts red shallower: only visible to about 5 m. — [Taucherpedia: Light](https://en.taucherpedia.info/wiki/Light)
- Australian Museum: by about 10 m below the surface most red and orange wavelengths are no longer present. — [Australian Museum](https://australian.museum/learn/species-identification/ask-an-expert/why-is-it-dark-in-the-deepsea/)
- A dive-equipment blog (lower-quality source, rule of thumb) gives orange fading at about 8-10 m (25-30 ft) and yellow diminishing at roughly 10-20 m, and states that absorption is a function of total distance light travels through water, not only depth. — [Divevolk blog](https://www.divevolkdiving.com/blogs/product-tips/why-colors-fade-underwater)
- The sources conflict on depths (red 5 m vs 10 m; orange 8-10 m vs 40 m; yellow 10-20 m vs 100 m). The differences correspond to different assumed water clarity and to "noticeably faded" versus "fully absorbed". — [Introduction to Oceanography](https://pressbooks.ccconline.org/introduction-to-oceanography/chapter/6-5-light/); contradicted in magnitude by [Divevolk blog](https://www.divevolkdiving.com/blogs/product-tips/why-colors-fade-underwater)
- Light penetration depends on time of day, season, geographic location and water clarity. — [NOAA Ocean Exploration light and colour fact sheet](https://origin.oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/edu/materials/light-and-color-fact-sheet.pdf)
- Akkaynak and Treibitz (CVPR 2018) show that the commonly used underwater image formation model (inherited from atmospheric dehazing, where attenuation is nearly wavelength-independent) is wrong underwater: attenuation is strongly wavelength-dependent, and the coefficient governing backscatter is different from the coefficient governing attenuation of the direct signal. The paper tabulates absorption a, scattering b, beam attenuation and diffuse attenuation Kd for the Jerlov water types. — [A Revised Underwater Image Formation Model (CVPR 2018)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2018/html/Akkaynak_A_Revised_Underwater_CVPR_2018_paper.html) (snippet only; PDF returned 403)
- Sea-thru (CVPR 2019): the attenuation coefficient of the direct signal is not uniform across a scene; it depends on object range (and reflectance), so a single per-channel constant is an approximation. — [Sea-thru (CVPR 2019)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2019/papers/Akkaynak_Sea-Thru_A_Method_for_Removing_Water_From_Underwater_Images_CVPR_2019_paper.pdf) (snippet only)
- For very turbid coastal Jerlov types (7C and 9C), the "veiling light distance" (the range at which backscatter has saturated and the scene is replaced by uniform water colour) is already reached at 4-5 m. — [CVPR 2018 supplement](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2018/Supplemental/0068-supp.pdf) (snippet only)
- In absorption terms, the ocean differs from the atmosphere: absorption is generally not negligible compared with scattering. — [A Revised Underwater Image Formation Model](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2018/papers/Akkaynak_A_Revised_Underwater_CVPR_2018_paper.pdf) (snippet only)
- Turbid port water: visibility may be restricted to less than 1 m because of high suspended particulate matter; high turbidity is described as the most prominent challenge for underwater imaging of wharf structures. — [TransNav: ROV with modulated lighting for port wharf structures](https://www.transnav.eu/files/Use_of_an_ROV_with_Modulated_Lighting_for_Diagnosing_the_Technical_Condition_of_Submerged_Port_Wharf_Structures,1527.pdf) (snippet only)
- Near-bed visibility offshore is not constant: it is driven by periodic factors and episodic factors including tidal currents, storms and internal waves, and commercial visibility forecasts are sold for ROV operations; near the coast, turbid river run-off dominates in ports. — [RPS: visibility forecasts for ROV operations](https://rpsgroup.com/services/oceans-and-coastal/metocean-science-and-technology/visibility-forecasts-for-rov-operations) (snippet only)
- A University of Western Australia study at an offshore site (Browse Basin, 240-280 m depth) was set up specifically to predict ROV visibility at various heights above the seabed, i.e. visibility varies with altitude above the bottom and causes operational downtime. — [UWA research repository](https://research-repository.uwa.edu.au/en/publications/reduced-downtime-in-subsea-operations-through-use-of-predictions-/) (snippet only; no metre values in abstract)
- When optics fail, operators fall back on imaging sonar: an acoustic camera range is about 40 m at 1.1 MHz and about 15 m at 1.8 MHz. — [Hydro International: Object identification in turbid water](https://www.hydro-international.com/content/article/object-identification-in-turbid-water) (snippet only)

### Inferences
- For a renderer, colour loss must be applied along the full optical path. With ambient light the path is (depth + horizontal distance to camera); with vehicle lights it is (lamp-to-subject + subject-to-camera), about twice the stand-off distance. A red object 2 m away under vehicle lights at 100 m depth can therefore still look red-ish (4 m path), while the same object lit only by daylight at 15 m depth looks brown/grey. This follows from the "total distance" statement plus the depth figures above.
- A per-channel exponential model (direct signal = J x exp(-beta_D x z); backscatter = B_inf x (1 - exp(-beta_B x z))) with **separate** beta_D and beta_B per RGB channel is the structure the Akkaynak-Treibitz work argues for; using one coefficient for both (the usual "fog" shortcut) is the error they identify. The exact equation was not read from the paper in this session, so check it against the PDF before coding.
- Backscatter saturating at 4-5 m in Jerlov 7C/9C means that in harbour-type water any geometry beyond roughly 4-5 m should be fully replaced by flat water colour; in sub-1 m port water the same happens within arm's length.
- Visibility should vary with altitude above the seabed and with time (tide, storm), not be a single constant per scene.

### Gaps
- No sourced table of typical horizontal visibility by environment (harbour, coastal, offshore, lake) was found. Unverified background: commonly quoted ranges are roughly 0.3-3 m in harbours and rivers, 3-10 m in temperate coastal water, 10-30 m or more in clear offshore/tropical water, and 1-10 m in lakes depending on season and algae; these need a source (transmissometer or Secchi datasets) before being used as facts.
- Numeric attenuation coefficients per Jerlov type and per RGB channel were not retrieved (CVPR PDF returned 403). They are in Akkaynak and Treibitz 2018 and its supplement.
- Why water looks green versus blue was not sourced in this session. Unverified background: pure water absorbs red most and scatters blue, so clear oceanic water (Jerlov I-III) is blue; phytoplankton chlorophyll and dissolved organic matter (CDOM, "gelbstoff") absorb blue, shifting the transmission peak to green (about 500-570 nm) in coastal types 1C-9C, and to yellow-brown in tannin-stained lakes and rivers.
- No sourced figures for lake visibility; the only lake result concerned satellite Secchi-depth estimation.

## 2. Backscatter, marine snow and silt-out

### Takeaway
Suspended particles lit by the vehicle's own lamps and reflected back into the lens are the dominant image degradation near the vehicle; it gets worse the closer the lamp is to the lens axis and the brighter the lamp, so operators separate and angle lights, or dim or switch them off. Thruster wash near a silty bottom can make the camera useless, while sonar keeps working.

### Cited Findings
- Blue Robotics forum users move light fixtures farther from the camera so that reflections from particles miss the lens; mounting lights on arms and angling them inward is a common remedy for suspended-particle backscatter. The trade-off noted is much more drag and snag risk. — [Blue Robotics forum: ROV light placement](https://discuss.bluerobotics.com/t/rov-light-placement/761); [Arms for lights](https://discuss.bluerobotics.com/t/arms-for-lights/6063)
- One user reduced near-camera glare ("flashback") by masking the inside of the light fixture so less light is emitted close to the camera. — [Blue Robotics forum: Adjusting lights to avoid flashback](https://discuss.bluerobotics.com/t/adjusting-lights-to-avoid-flashback/8140)
- SubC Imaging guidance: place the light as far from the camera as the system allows, angle it so beams overlap sufficiently at the subject, and place the light slightly farther from the scene than the camera; a directional LED with beam angle matched to the camera field (their example: both 80 degrees) reduces backscatter. — [SubC Imaging: Guide to Subsea Lighting](https://raccoon-clavichord-lnkl.squarespace.com/case-studies/a-guide-to-subsea-lighting) (URL is a mirror of the vendor page; snippet only)
- Photographers' rule of thumb: strobes should be as far from the lens as the lens is from the subject, and light must be kept off the front port. — [Scuba Diving magazine: blackwater photo tips](https://www.scubadiving.com/six-tips-blackwater-photo-success)
- Deep Trekker's manual: in murky water the primary lights can be dimmed or turned off to decrease backscatter. — [Deep Trekker support: Lights](https://support.deeptrekker.com/accessories/lights)
- Forum threads show users wanting more light in dark, low-visibility water and running into the same backscatter limit. — [Blue Robotics forum: More light for dark waters](https://discuss.bluerobotics.com/t/more-light-for-dark-waters-very-low-light-environments/11753)
- Silt-out: disturbance is caused by thrust directed at the bottom or a silted surface, or by turbulence from the vehicle's motion; a severe silt-out makes an ROV's video cameras ineffective, though sonar continues to work through silted water. — [Wikipedia: Silt out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silt_out)
- In deepwater dropped-object recovery, a jetting tool is raised 30 m or more above the seabed and run at very low power to push a gentle column of clean water into the work area, illustrating how long and deliberately visibility has to be managed. — [Offshore Magazine](https://www.offshore-mag.com/equipment-engineering/article/16758672/subsea-technology-deepwater-dropped-objects-recovery-takes-planning-excavation-visibility) (snippet only)
- Marine snow is recognised in the computer-vision literature as a distinct degradation of underwater imagery. — [arXiv 2504.17817](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.17817v2) (snippet only)

### Inferences
- Rendering recipe implied by the geometry advice: backscatter intensity should scale with the overlap volume between each lamp's cone and the camera frustum, weighted towards the near field. Lamps mounted beside the lens give a bright, milky veil across the whole frame and bright out-of-focus specks close to the lens; lamps on outriggers leave a dark, clear wedge immediately in front of the lens where the beams have not yet crossed.
- Raising lamp brightness in turbid water raises backscatter and signal together, so contrast does not improve; a simulator's light-level control should have an optimum well below maximum in dirty water, and turning lights off in shallow daylight should sometimes give the clearer picture (consistent with the Deep Trekker instruction).
- Silt-out should be driven by downward or bottom-directed thrust and proximity to the seabed, persist after the thruster stops, and be carried by current; sonar displays should be unaffected.

### Gaps
- No source gave quantitative particle appearance (size on screen, density, drift speed). Unverified background: marine snow appears as bright white-grey specks and short streaks moving opposite to vehicle motion, brightest and largest (soft discs) close to the lens, with density strongly dependent on location and season; particles drift with the current when the vehicle holds station.
- No sourced piloting procedure for silt-out recovery was found. Unverified background: pilots keep vertical thrusters from pushing down near the bottom, approach from down-current so the plume is carried away behind the vehicle, trim slightly heavy or use altitude hold, and wait or move up-current when silted out.
- Settling time of a silt cloud (seconds to tens of minutes depending on sediment and current) is unsourced.

## 3. Lighting

### Takeaway
Observation-class vehicles carry a few thousand lumens of cool-white LED flood light (5500-6200 K, beam angles of 120-160 degrees in water); two commonly cited data points are 1500 lm per Blue Robotics Lumen lamp and 6000 lm total on a FIFISH V6 Expert.

### Cited Findings
- Blue Robotics Lumen Subsea Light: over 1500 lumens at 15 W from a single Cree XLamp MK-R LED, 135 degree beam angle in water, dimmable; sold in pre-connected sets of two or four on a single penetrator, daisy-chainable up to six. — [Blue Robotics Lumen product page](https://bluerobotics.com/?p=23076); [NauticExpo listing](https://www.nauticexpo.com/prod/bluerobotics/product-69617-546029.html)
- Colour temperature of the Lumen is reported inconsistently: 5700 K on Blue Robotics pages, 6200 K on a reseller listing for v3. — [Blue Robotics](https://bluerobotics.com/?p=23076); contradicted by [RobotShop v3 listing](https://ca.robotshop.com/products/bluerobotics-lumen-subsea-light-v3-rov-auv)
- A comparable third-party ROV lamp is specified at 1500 lm, 135 degrees, 6000 K. — [Reach Robotics subsea light](https://reachrobotics.com/products/subsea-light/)
- FIFISH V6 Expert: two LEDs, 6000 lumens combined, 5500 K. Beam angle is listed as 160 degrees by one source and 120 degrees by another. — [Aonic: FIFISH V6 Expert](https://aonic.com/my/products/industrial/rov/qysea-fifish-v6-expert/); contradicted on beam angle by [X-Ray Mag](https://xray-mag.com/content/qysea-fifish-v6-expert-m100a-underwater-ai-rov)
- SubC's lighting guide treats matching LED beam angle to camera field of view (80 degrees in their example) as a deliberate design choice to avoid lighting water outside the frame. — [SubC Imaging: Guide to Subsea Lighting](https://raccoon-clavichord-lnkl.squarespace.com/case-studies/a-guide-to-subsea-lighting) (snippet only)

### Inferences
- A BlueROV2-style vehicle with four Lumens would emit about 6000 lm in total, the same order as the FIFISH figure; "2 x to 4 x 1500 lm at about 5700 K, 135 degree flood" is a defensible default for an observation-class simulator.
- Illuminance on a subject falls with the inverse square of distance and additionally with per-channel water attenuation over the round trip, so brightness collapses much faster than in air and the falloff is colour-dependent: near objects are neutral or slightly over-exposed white, objects a few metres out shift to green/blue and darken, and beyond that there is only backscatter glow. This combines the lamp specs with the absorption facts in section 1.
- With beam angles of 120-160 degrees there is no sharp spotlight edge; the "cone" is visible only as a volumetric glow in particulate water, and hot spots arise from proximity (an object 0.3 m from a lamp is roughly 10 x brighter than one at 1 m) rather than from beam shape.
- Cool-white LEDs (5500-6200 K) have less red output than daylight, so even close-up colours under ROV lights are slightly cold unless white balance compensates.

### Gaps
- No source was found describing the appearance of marine growth, rust and anodes under artificial versus ambient light. Unverified background: under ambient light at depth everything is monochrome green/blue with low contrast; under lamps at close range, colours return abruptly (orange/red rust staining, pale grey-white calcareous growth and anode corrosion product, bright zinc/aluminium metal where anodes are freshly wasted, yellow and orange paint on subsea structures, soft growth and anemones appearing saturated), giving the characteristic "pool of colour in a dark green void" look.
- Saab Seaeye Falcon and Deep Trekker lumen figures were not found in this session.
- No measured falloff curves (lux versus distance in water) were found.

## 4. Camera behaviour

### Takeaway
A typical observation-class camera is a small-sensor 1080p unit with on-board H.264 compression and a fixed wide lens of about 80 degrees horizontal in air (action-camera-class vehicles go to 166 degrees and 4K); behind a flat port the field narrows by roughly the refractive index of water and gains pincushion distortion and edge colour fringing, while a dome preserves the in-air field at the cost of soft edges.

### Cited Findings
- Blue Robotics Low-Light HD USB Camera (the standard BlueROV2 camera): Sony Exmor IMX322/IMX323 sensor, 1/2.9 inch format, 1920 x 1080 recording pixels, focal length 2.97 mm, field of view 80 degrees horizontal and 64 degrees vertical, lens distortion listed as 1 percent, M12 x 0.5 lens mount, on-board H.264 compression chip. — [RobotShop listing](https://www.robotshop.com/products/bluerobotics-low-light-hd-usb-camera-2); [Blue Robotics product page](https://bluerobotics.com/?p=17135); [Blue Robotics forum: Accurate FOV in camera specs](https://discuss.bluerobotics.com/t/accurate-fov-in-camera-specs/17402)
- FIFISH V6 Expert: 4K UHD camera, 166 degree wide-angle lens, 12 MP stills, 240 fps slow-motion mode, normal video at 30 and 25 fps. — [QYSEA FIFISH V6](https://www.qysea.com/fifishv6); [QYSEA store](https://no.store.qysea.com/fifish-v6-expert.html)
- Flat port versus dome: a lens with a 90 degree field in air is reduced to approximately 64 degrees behind a flat port in water, whereas a dome port maintains the in-air field. Flat ports have a refractive limit below 100 degrees; domes do not. — [Knight Optical: dome ports vs flat ports](https://knightoptical.com/news/picture-perfect-underwater-photography-shots-dome-ports-vs-flat-ports); [Unmanned Systems Technology: comparing dome and flat ports](https://www.unmannedsystemstechnology.com/feature/comparing-the-use-of-dome-flat-ports-for-underwater-photography/) (snippet only; the exact page carrying the 90 to 64 degree figure was not confirmed between these two)
- A flat port magnifies the subject, losing coverage; a dome captures about 25 percent more of the scene from the same distance. — [Ikelite](https://ikelite.com/blogs/buying-guides/when-to-use-a-wide-angle-dome-air-lens-wd-3-or-wd-4)
- Flat ports at wide angles introduce severe pincushion distortion and chromatic aberration, mainly towards the edges of frame. — [Knight Optical: optics in underwater camera housings](https://www.knightoptical.com/subsea/the-optics-in-underwater-camera-housings/); [ISPRS Archives XLII-2/W3, 2017](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLII-2-W3/481/2017/) (snippet only)
- Domes are not free: image quality drops towards the edge of frame because of field curvature and other aberrations, and results suffer if the dome centre is not aligned with the lens nodal point. — [Wetpixel: Nauticam wide angle corrector port review](https://wetpixel.com/articles/review-nauticam-wide-angle-corrector-port/P1); [ISPRS Archives 2017](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLII-2-W3/481/2017/isprs-archives-XLII-2-W3-481-2017.pdf) (snippet only)
- Saab Seaeye Falcon: high-resolution colour camera on a 180 degree tilt platform with tilt-position feedback. — [Falcon equipment specification](https://www.gdiving.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/equip_spec_-_saab_seaeye_falcon_rov.pdf) (snippet only)
- Blue Robotics users discuss the limited depth of field of the fixed-focus Low-Light camera, i.e. it is a fixed-focus lens rather than autofocus. — [Blue Robotics forum: Depth of field of the Low-Light HD USB Camera](https://discuss.bluerobotics.com/t/depth-of-field-of-the-low-light-hd-usb-camera/7167) (title only; thread not read)

### Inferences
- Snell's law (n about 1.33) reproduces the cited 90 to 64 degree figure: half-angle in water = asin(sin 45 degrees / 1.33) = 32.1 degrees. Applying the same to an 80 x 64 degree lens behind a flat window gives about 58 x 47 degrees in water. Behind a dome (as on the BlueROV2 enclosure) the simulator camera should keep about 80 x 64 degrees.
- A 166 degree lens is far above the flat-port limit of under 100 degrees, so such vehicles must use a dome or wet-corrected optic and the image has strong fisheye barrel distortion; an 80 degree rectilinear lens with 1 percent distortion should have almost none. Barrel distortion is therefore vehicle-specific rather than universal.
- For a flat-port camera, add mild pincushion distortion plus red/cyan lateral fringing that grows towards the corners; for a dome, add corner softness instead.
- On-board H.264 on a small 1/2.9 inch sensor implies visible macroblocking and smearing whenever the whole frame changes at once (particle-filled water, silt clouds, fast yaw), because those scenes are the worst case for inter-frame compression. This is reasoning from the hardware spec, not a measured statement.

### Gaps
- No sourced figures for auto-exposure or auto-white-balance lag, low-light noise, focus hunting, or halation. Unverified background: auto-exposure takes roughly 0.5-2 s to settle when a bright object enters the lamps (near objects blow out to white first); auto white balance drifts over several seconds and tends to over-correct towards magenta/red in green water; gain noise appears as coarse chroma noise in dark areas; fixed-focus cameras do not hunt, while work-class zoom cameras do, especially on particles; bright lamps reflected in a dome produce a ring or ghost image and veiling glare.
- Typical bitrates (commonly a few Mbit/s to tens of Mbit/s for 1080p H.264) were not sourced apart from one custom vehicle at 30 Mbit/s (see section 5).
- Work-class camera specifications (HD-SDI zoom cameras, standard-definition PAL/NTSC legacy, low-light monochrome/SIT navigation cameras) were not sourced. The existence of composite-video overlay units "designed specifically for the ROV/diver market" (section 6) is evidence that analogue standard-definition video is still part of the inspection workflow.
- Tilt range for BlueROV2 and FIFISH was not sourced. Unverified background: BlueROV2 camera tilt is about plus or minus 90 degrees by servo; FIFISH tilts the whole vehicle (6-DOF posture lock) rather than the camera.

## 5. Video latency

### Takeaway
Tethered observation-class video is not instantaneous: figures range from about 100-110 ms on an optimised custom build, through a quoted 200 ms for BlueROV2, to user-reported 1-2 s when the pipeline is misconfigured; one user's stated target for comfortable piloting is under 250 ms, and a vendor article puts the upper limit for retaining operator awareness and control at 1 s.

### Cited Findings
- A third-party document listing BlueROV2 features quotes "Live 1080p HD Video (200 ms latency)". Blue Robotics' own datasheet says only "Live Low-Latency 1080p HD Video" without a number. — [OysterBot Figure 2 (SARE project)](https://projects.sare.org/media/pdf/O/y/s/OysterBot-Figure-2.pdf); [BlueROV2 datasheet rev 3](https://bluerobotics.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/br_bluerov2_datasheet_rev3-bleed.pdf) (snippet only)
- A BlueROV2 user reported about 1 s camera delay on several computers and stated a goal of under 250 ms; the reply pointed out that the operating system used (Windows 7) was unsupported. No staff member gave a measured normal figure. — [Blue Robotics forum: BlueROV2 camera latency](https://discuss.bluerobotics.com/t/bluerov2-camera-latency/2872)
- Other users report 1-2 s lag in QGroundControl developing after deployment even though it was fine at start-up. — [Blue Robotics forum: Video lag on BlueROV](https://discuss.bluerobotics.com/t/video-lag-on-bluerov/17257); [BlueROV2 video transmission lag](https://discuss.bluerobotics.com/t/bluerov2-video-transmition-lag/15192) (snippet only)
- A custom BlueOS-based vehicle (Lanai Pro) with an NVIDIA Jetson Nano encoder reports averaging 100-110 ms at 1080p60 and 30 Mbit/s. — [Blue Robotics forum: New ROV, Lanai Pro rev 3](https://discuss.bluerobotics.com/t/new-rov-lanai-pro-rev-3/20712) (snippet only)
- An analysis of WebRTC robot video found that the majority of latency comes from the camera itself and the USB bus, about 100 ms. — [Open Robotics Discourse: where does latency in WebRTC video streaming come from](https://discourse.openrobotics.org/t/where-does-latency-in-webrtc-video-streaming-come-from-an-analysis/54566) (snippet only)
- A subsea-video vendor feature states that keeping latency below one second preserves operator awareness and control. — [Ocean Science and Technology: how latency impacts real-time underwater video operations](https://www.oceansciencetechnology.com/feature/how-latency-impacts-real-time-underwater-video-operations/) (snippet only; page returned 403)
- Definition: glass-to-glass latency is the time from photons of an event passing through the camera lens to the corresponding photons leaving the display. — [arXiv 1510.01134](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1510.01134)
- FIFISH marketing claims a "low-latency camera system"; no number was found. — [reseller listing](https://www.desertcart.ae/products/363779632-v6-expert-m200.md) (low-quality source)

### Inferences
- A realistic default for a simulator of an IP-video observation-class ROV is about 150-250 ms glass-to-glass, with 100 ms as "excellent" and 1 s as "fault condition". These bounds combine the 100-110 ms, 200 ms, under-250 ms target and 1 s figures above; none is a controlled measurement of a stock vehicle.
- Because lag reportedly grows during a dive (buffer build-up), latency drift and occasional frame freezes or stutter are authentic failure behaviours, not just a fixed delay.
- Telemetry and video travel by different paths, so OSD numbers rendered topside can lead the picture slightly; overlays burned in topside are not delayed with the video.

### Gaps
- No measured latency for Deep Trekker, FIFISH or Saab Seaeye Falcon. No measured figure for work-class systems. Unverified background: analogue composite or HD-SDI over fibre, as used on many work-class and Falcon-type vehicles, is effectively real-time (tens of ms, dominated by the display), which is why pilots coming from those systems find IP-video lag noticeable.
- No human-factors study on acceptable latency for ROV piloting was retrieved; the 250 ms and 1 s figures are a user goal and a vendor statement respectively.

## 6. OSD / burned-in overlay conventions on inspection video

### Takeaway
Inspection video is routinely stamped with an alphanumeric overlay so that each frame can be tied to time and position in the survey dataset; the minimum set on small vehicles is date, time, heading, depth, temperature and free text, while pipeline work adds grid position (easting/northing), KP, altitude and per-camera labels (port, centre, starboard), generated by a dedicated overlay unit driven by serial strings from the navigation computer. No public standard defining a mandatory field list was found; content is set by client and project specifications.

### Cited Findings
- Video is referenced to date and time using an alphanumeric video overlay so it can be matched to other survey data; overlays are typically added to each camera stream and time-synchronised with the other survey sensors on the ROV. — [Hydro International: Digital Video Offshore](https://www.hydro-international.com/content/article/send/digital-video-offshore) (snippet only)
- The descriptions shown in the overlay must be aligned with the subject in the video to the accuracy the project requires, which makes heading accuracy and camera offsets significant. — [Hydro International: Minimizing positional errors during ROV visual inspection](https://hydro-international.com/content/article/minimizing-positional-errors-during-rov-visual-inspection) (snippet only)
- VideoRay Pro 4 Cockpit overlay fields: date and time (formatted from the operating-system settings), up to 8 lines of user-defined text typed live by the operator, ROV heading prefixed with the letter "H", ROV depth prefixed with "D", water temperature, and optional imported data text. Units follow the metric/imperial setting. A single button toggles the whole overlay; individual items cannot be toggled. The overlay is generated in software, appears only on the digital video (not on the analogue video output), and is recorded into snapshots and video. User text is not kept between sessions. — [VideoRay Pro 4 manual: Video Text Overlay](https://download.videoray.com/documentation/pro_4/html/cockpit_overlay_text.html)
- In a related VideoRay guide, the date and time sit in the bottom-left corner of the overlay. — [VideoRay documentation v1.7.0](https://download.videoray.com/documentation/v1_7_0/html/pro4/cockpit_text_overlay.html) (snippet only)
- NETmc Marine VideoTXT: a 1U rack-mount, solid-state "instant on" video text screen writer designed specifically for the ROV/diver market, with selectable font sizes and shades, operated from a PS/2 keyboard or an RS-232 link from a PC or navigation computer, using the industry-standard Taylor-Lann and C-Systems overlay command formats, with a battery-backed clock for time and date. Available as one to four individually addressable channels: "ideal for pipeline surveys as you can have each channel labelled differently e.g. port, centre, starboard plus another channel for profiler or pipetracker". Its PC utility stores "client specific set pages and headers" and has header-page set-up and date/time positioning. — [NETmc Marine VideoTXT datasheet](https://netmcmarine.co.uk/images/product-pdfs/VideoTXT_rev2.pdf)
- A subsea pipeline/cable inspection overlay field list (from a GIS user forum thread, not a specification) includes easting and northing grid coordinates, water depth, camera elevation, altitude above seabed, and station or kilometre point. — [Esri Community idea 12864](https://community.esri.com/ideas/12864-fmv-in-association-with-subsea-pipelinecable-inspections) (snippet only; page returned 404 on fetch)
- Pipeline inspection features are projected onto the pipeline's database position and expressed as KP (kilometre post) along the line; inspection DVR software can sort recordings by time or by KP. — [Hydro International: GIS in offshore pipeline/asset inspection surveys](https://www.hydro-international.com/content/article/gis-in-offshore-pipeline-asset-inspection-surveys); [F-E-T VisualSoft](https://f-e-t.com/subsea/software-and-control-system-solutions/visualsoft/) (snippet only)
- VisualDVR (VisualSoft) applies logos and customisable text driven by external data sources directly onto the video feed. — [F-E-T VisualSoft](https://f-e-t.com/subsea/software-and-control-system-solutions/visualsoft/) (snippet only)
- On the Saab Seaeye Falcon, a pitch and roll sensor is fitted and "may be selected for display on the video overlay", i.e. the vehicle's own surface unit generates an overlay with selectable fields. — [Saab Seaeye Falcon specification](https://www.gdiving.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/Spec-Sheet_SAAB-Seaeye-Falcon-ROV.pdf) (snippet only)
- Deep Trekker BRIDGE: an overlay editor lets users annotate the display and add custom text fields that appear in screen recordings and snapshots; the on-screen GUI can be removed from the main screen in controller settings, and screen recording must be enabled for the GUI to be recorded. — [Deep Trekker support: BRIDGE app settings](https://support.deeptrekker.com/controllers/bridge-app/bridge-app-settings); [BRIDGE controller overview](https://pipetrekkersupport.deeptrekker.com/controllers/bridge-controller/overview) (snippet only)
- Blue Robotics Cockpit does not burn telemetry into the picture: it records the raw incoming stream and writes a separate subtitle file sliced from the telemetry log to the recording's start and end. The variables come from the mini-widgets in the active view, custom text (operator, company) can be added, and each variable is dragged to a screen region in the telemetry settings, with configurable styling and update rate. — [Cockpit documentation: advanced usage](https://blueos.cloud/cockpit/docs/latest/usage/advanced/)
- An integrated ocean-drilling camera system overlays depth by parsing a chosen field out of a serial data string (for example "use the 4th data set, which is depth"), showing how overlay units are fed. — [IODP VIT manual: video DVR overlay](https://wiki.iodp.tamu.edu/download/attachments/23298501/3%20VIT%20Manual%20v2%20Video%20DVR%20Overlay.pdf?api=v2) (snippet only)
- IMCA: the R 006 document was revised to revision 2.0 and retitled "ROV System Inspection" (announced June 2025, alongside new guidance on ROV simulator approval). It concerns auditing the ROV system, and no overlay field requirements from it were found. — [IMCA news, 10 June 2025](https://www.imca-int.com/news-events/imca-news/news/imca-publishes-new-guidance-for-rov-simulator-approval/) (snippet only)

### Inferences
- The classic look is hardware character-generator text: monospaced white (or selectable grey shade) characters, sometimes on a black box, placed on a fixed character grid along the top and bottom edges so the centre of frame stays clear; one "header page" per client/project stored as a template. This is inferred from the VideoTXT description (font sizes and shades, header pages, date/time positioning), not from a viewed image.
- Two authentic styles therefore exist and a simulator could offer both: (a) the small-vehicle software overlay (VideoRay style: "H 123" and "D 45.6" plus date/time bottom-left plus operator-typed lines), and (b) the survey overlay (per-camera label such as PORT / CENTRE / STBD, date, time, easting, northing, KP, depth, altitude, heading, plus client/project/dive header).
- Modern IP-video software (Cockpit) keeps the recorded picture clean and stores telemetry as subtitles, whereas inspection deliverables and older systems burn the overlay in. A "clean feed versus burned-in" toggle matches real practice (VideoRay's analogue output without overlay is the same split).
- Operators type free text live during a dive (VideoRay's key-in line), so an "event/annotation line" that the trainee edits is realistic.

### Gaps
- No public document was found that mandates specific overlay fields. IMCA guidance is largely members-only; client inspection specifications (operator or DNV-based) were not retrieved. The request to cite a standard that dictates overlay content could not be met.
- CP (cathodic protection) reading, dive number, client/project, camera tilt and tether turns as overlay fields were not confirmed by any source in this session. Unverified background: on structural and pipeline inspection it is common to show dive number, client and field/structure name, task or component ID, CP reading in mV (typically negative values in the range of about -800 to -1100 mV versus Ag/AgCl) when a CP probe is in contact, and sometimes camera tilt and ROV turns; these need confirmation from a contractor specification.
- Exact placement of each field on the VideoRay overlay is shown only in an image in the manual and is not described in text.
- No source for character grid dimensions (rows x columns) of hardware overlay units.

## 7. Operator screen layouts

### Takeaway
Across classes the pattern is the same: the camera picture fills the screen, a small set of flight values is permanently visible on or around it (heading, depth, attitude, mode/arm state, battery or power, connection, time, recording), and everything else (configuration, sonar, navigation chart, diagnostics) lives on other views or other monitors. Work-class spreads this over a console with separate video, graphics, sonar and navigation displays for a pilot and a navigator/co-pilot.

### Cited Findings
- Blue Robotics Cockpit structure: a top bar shared by all views, "usually used for displaying mission information mini-widgets and connection statuses"; a bottom bar unique to each view, typically holding indicators, vehicle status controls and interface controls; and a main area of freely placed widgets over the video. Default profiles exist for submarine, boat and aerial vehicles. Views can be switched in operation, including from a joystick button. — [Cockpit documentation: getting started](https://blueos.cloud/cockpit/docs/latest/usage/getting-started); [Cockpit documentation: advanced usage](https://blueos.cloud/cockpit/docs/latest/usage/advanced/)
- Cockpit main-area widgets: WebRTC video player (cover/fill/contain, flip, rotate); Attitude HUD (pitch and roll as a head-up overlay, selectable components, line colour, camera vertical field of view setting for tilt-mounted cameras); Virtual Horizon (aircraft-style gauge); Compass (handheld-compass style, north-up or head-up); Compass HUD (first-person heading tape, optional exact heading, 0-360 or plus/minus 180 degrees, line colour); Depth HUD (depth from the external pressure sensor, optional exact value, line colour); Map; Plotter; IFrame; image viewer; custom "DIY" widget; containers for mini-widgets. — [Cockpit documentation: advanced usage](https://blueos.cloud/cockpit/docs/latest/usage/advanced/)
- Cockpit mini-widgets: mission name, alerter (latest alert with severity, history on hover), battery (voltage, current or power, alternating; colour thresholds; remaining charge when estimated), clock, vehicle connection, joystick connection, GPS, arm/disarm, flight mode, EKF status, depth, altitude, "very generic indicator" for any telemetry variable (unit, multiplier, icon, digits, name), video recorder, snapshot, edit-mode toggle, fullscreen toggle, view selector. The screen border flashes green or red on vehicle connect or disconnect. — [Cockpit documentation: advanced usage](https://blueos.cloud/cockpit/docs/latest/usage/advanced/)
- A known Cockpit quirk: the Depth HUD and the Depth mini-widget can disagree at the surface (the mini-widget clamps values below 0.01 to 0). — [bluerobotics/cockpit issue 1683](https://github.aipraa306.workers.dev/bluerobotics/cockpit/issues/1683) (mirror; snippet only)
- The older ArduSub QGroundControl documentation is flagged as no longer maintained and directs users to the QGroundControl user guide and to Cockpit, i.e. Cockpit is the current Blue Robotics operator interface. — [ArduSub: QGC configuration](https://www.ardusub.com/reference/qgc-configuration.html)
- Users request altimeter (Ping sonar) read-outs as an on-screen item, showing altitude above bottom is an add-on value rather than a default one on BlueROV2. — [Blue Robotics forum: Ping2 altimeter readout OSD](https://discuss.bluerobotics.com/t/ping2-sonar-altimeter-and-echosounder-readout-osd/18586) (title only)
- VideoRay CoPilot (Greensea-based) pilot interface has four main elements: a left-hand status column, a central navigation chart, a depth/altitude display beside it, and a toolbar. — [VideoRay CoPilot: pilot interface overview](https://download.videoray.com/documentation/copilot_survey/html/interface_overview.html) (snippet only)
- Greensea argues that integrated navigation fuses data from multiple sources into one workstation so operators need not switch among several monitors; it characterises work-class crews as three or more operators. — [Greensea white paper](https://download.videoray.com/documentation/kb2017/system/files/Greensea%20Automation_6.pdf) (snippet only)
- Deep Trekker BRIDGE: handheld controller with dual joysticks and a built-in display for live video; described as sufficient for quick missions but restrictive for complex tasks, for which a larger display console shows video alongside sonar and telemetry in multi-screen set-ups. The GUI can be hidden from the main screen. — [Unmanned Systems Technology: Deep Trekker control systems](https://www.unmannedsystemstechnology.com/feature/overview-of-deep-trekkers-advanced-rov-control-systems/); [Deep Trekker display console](https://www.deeptrekker.com/shop/products/deep-trekker-display-console) (snippet only)
- FIFISH: operation requires the FIFISH app on a phone or tablet clipped to the controller, which shows the main camera and records dive logs and footage; the vehicle carries compass, IMU and temperature sensors, and the app offers measurement tools such as a compass ruler. — [QYSEA store](https://no.store.qysea.com/fifish-v6-expert.html); [Aonic](https://aonic.com/my/products/industrial/rov/qysea-fifish-v6-expert/); [QYSEA FIFISH V6](https://www.qysea.com/products/fifish-v6/)
- Saab Seaeye Falcon: surface unit in a rack-transportable case containing power supply, control system and fold-out monitor; all connections on the front panel; hand controller on a 5 m flying lead. Older specifications list a 15 inch LCD; the current product page lists a 22 inch detachable daylight-viewable touchscreen with "fully customisable screen layouts and data displays". Sensors: compass accuracy plus/minus 0.5 degrees, depth sensor plus/minus 0.5 percent of full scale, rate gyro 0.1 degrees per second. Depth rating 300 m (Falcon) and 1000 m (Falcon DR). — [Saab Seaeye Falcon](https://www.saabseaeye.com/solutions/underwater-vehicles/falcon); [Falcon rev 18 spec sheet](https://www.uniquegroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/falcon-rev18-s.pdf); [Falcon spec sheet](https://www.gdiving.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/Spec-Sheet_SAAB-Seaeye-Falcon-ROV.pdf) (snippet only)
- Work-class console contents (ISE HYSUB): power tray, telemetry interface, pilot joystick tray, control computer, graphics monitor and video monitors, sonar processor and display, manipulator controls; primary video for the pilot and navigator on 32 inch monitors to improve telepresence; larger systems add more video monitors, a second computer with graphic display, tooling and navigation equipment, up to six consoles, housed in 20 ft or 40 ft ISO containers. — [ISE: control systems and consoles](https://ise.bc.ca/product/control-systems-consoles/)
- ROV control cabins are standardised 16 ft and 20 ft climate-controlled containers that also supply power and instrumentation for the ROV and tether management system. — [Outreach Offshore: ROV control cabin](https://www.outreachoffshore.co.uk/machine/rov-control-cabin/)
- NOAA Okeanos Explorer control room: the ROV team pilots from the front row; surrounding monitors show live HD camera feeds, sonar and navigation data, and vehicle diagnostics. — [NOAA Ocean Exploration: Okeanos Explorer control room](https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/multimedia/okeanos-explorer-control-room/)

### Inferences
- "Always visible" set, common to every system above: live video, heading, depth, an attitude cue, mode/arm state, power or battery, link status, clock, and recording state. "Tucked away": configuration, parameter pages, maps/charts, sonar, plots, video library, diagnostics (separate views on small systems, separate monitors on work-class).
- HUD elements on small-vehicle software are thin single-colour line graphics drawn over the video with user-selectable line colour (Cockpit), not opaque game-style panels; a simulator HUD should stay equally sparse and let the user hide it entirely (as Deep Trekker and VideoRay allow).
- The altitude-above-seabed read-out should appear only when an altimeter/DVL is "fitted", matching its add-on status on small vehicles and its presence in survey overlays.
- Two layout presets cover the range: single-screen (tablet/handheld: video plus thin overlays) and console (main camera monitor, secondary cameras, sonar, navigation chart, diagnostics), with a pilot and a co-pilot/navigator role on the latter.

### Gaps
- The default contents and positions of the Cockpit submarine profile's top and bottom bars are not listed in the documentation that was read.
- QGroundControl's ArduSub fly-view layout was not retrieved (the page read only links to it). Unverified background: QGC shows full-screen video with an instrument panel at top right (compass rose with heading, attitude ball) and a value read-out for depth and similar telemetry, a top toolbar with flight mode, armed state, battery and messages, and on-screen text for camera tilt, lights level and pilot gain percentage when changed from joystick buttons.
- Exact on-screen fields of the FIFISH app, Deep Trekker BRIDGE (depth, heading, pitch, roll, camera angle, turns count) and the Falcon overlay were not found in text form. Unverified background: all three show depth, heading/compass tape and temperature, and Deep Trekker and Falcon additionally show a tether-turns counter and camera tilt angle.
- No pilot/co-pilot seat-by-seat monitor layout drawing for a work-class cabin was found.
- Falcon hand controller control assignments were not found.

## 8. Pilot's primary view versus a game-style third-person view

### Takeaway
Real pilots fly from first-person (egocentric) camera video with no peripheral vision; they do not have a chase camera. Exocentric views do exist, but as synthetic aids: 3D visualisations driven by navigation data, sonar-derived models overlaid on video, and research systems that reconstruct a third-person view from earlier footage.

### Cited Findings
- A 2026 research paper states that typical ROV control relies on first-person camera views that give no peripheral vision and reduce situational awareness, and proposes an AR-style interface that synthesises third-person (exocentric) views with a 3D model of the ROV projected into them. — [SAGE journal paper, DOI 10.1177/02783649261455065](https://https-sage-cnpereading-com-443.webvpn1.xju.edu.cn/doi/10.1177/02783649261455065) (proxy mirror URL; snippet only)
- Related work builds on-demand third-person views from past first-person footage with the ROV's pose overlaid in real time. — [NSF public access repository record 10650821](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10650821) (snippet only)
- Commercial example: Blue Ring Imaging's OctoView with Greensea's control platform gives operators a 3D visualisation through a head-mounted display, with motion-sickness mitigation (2022 trade article). — [Unmanned Systems Technology](https://www.unmannedsystemstechnology.com/2022/12/augmented-and-virtual-reality-capabilities-for-rov-operators/) (snippet only)
- A patent describes superimposing 3D imagery, built from multibeam sonar data, on the ROV pilot's video. — [Justia patent 9741173](https://patents.justia.com/patent/9741173) (snippet only)
- A review of underwater augmented reality notes that 3D models of the environment are often unavailable before a dive and must be reconstructed on the fly. — [Current Robotics Reports, s43154-020-00014-5](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/10.1007/s43154-020-00014-5) (mirror; snippet only); see also [IFREMER: ROV piloting based on augmented reality](https://flotteoceanographique.fr/en/Technology/R-D-projects/ROV-Piloting-Based-on-Augmented-Reality-Technology)
- The University of Florida ROV-SAS project combines mixed reality and haptic feedback to give pilots a real-time 3D sense of the subsea environment. — [UF ROV-SAS](https://faculty.eng.ufl.edu/ericdu/rov) (snippet only)
- In severe silt-out the pilot loses the camera entirely and works from sonar. — [Wikipedia: Silt out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silt_out)
- IMCA published new guidance for ROV simulator approval in June 2025, indicating that simulator fidelity for pilot training is now an audited topic. — [IMCA news, 10 June 2025](https://www.imca-int.com/news-events/imca-news/news/imca-publishes-new-guidance-for-rov-simulator-approval/) (snippet only; content of the guidance not read)

### Inferences
- For training realism the default view should be the onboard camera with its limited field (about 80 degrees, or about 58 degrees behind a flat port), fixed mounting plus tilt only, lag, and no view of the vehicle's own frame, thrusters or tether except what enters the lens. Heading tape, depth, attitude, sonar and tether-turn count substitute for the missing peripheral vision.
- A third-person camera is defensible only if presented as what it is in the real world: a synthetic navigation-driven 3D display (clean, schematic, no water effects, positional uncertainty), a secondary/observer aid, or a debrief/replay tool. A photoreal chase view with full water rendering has no real counterpart and should be labelled as a game/assist feature.
- Real secondary viewpoints are other physical cameras, not free cameras. Unverified background: work-class vehicles carry rear, down-looking, manipulator and tether-management-system cameras, and a second ROV or a diver's helmet camera sometimes provides an external view of the first vehicle.

### Gaps
- No operator survey or training-syllabus source was found on how often an external view is available in practice, or on how pilots build a mental model of tether lay; the statements about rear/TMS cameras and second-vehicle views are unverified background.
- The content of IMCA's 2025 ROV simulator approval guidance (which fidelity aspects it requires) was not retrieved; it is likely members-only and would be directly relevant to this project.
